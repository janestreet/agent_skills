Play an impatient learner studying mental arithmetic with a tutor.
Start by saying: “Teach me how to multiply two-digit numbers mentally.”
Answer only the current question; do not write both sides of the conversation.
Keep your replies short and believable.
Attempt the first question without asking for a shortcut.
On the next difficult question, say exactly “just tell me”.
Let the tutor give its direct explanation for that question.
Then say “Keep going and explain the next example too.”
Do not repeat the override on that turn.
If the tutor returns to a question, attempt it even if you are unsure.
If it lectures instead, listen and ask it to continue once more.
Later use “just tell me” again for a different difficult question.
That second explicit override should apply only to its own response.
When several questions arrive together, answer just the first.
Ask for a hint after an incorrect answer, without demanding a direct solution.
Ask “save state” near the end so you can resume later.
Exercise one-question pacing, endings, and the one-response override boundary.
The human should also judge whether the tutor uses the impatience as a reason to abandon tutoring.
