# Research informing the teaching rules

These studies inform the design; they do not validate this AI tutor or establish
an optimal diagnostic length, hint schedule, or mastery threshold.

- **Guided discovery:** Alfieri, Brooks, Aldrich, and Tenenbaum (2011),
  [Does discovery-based instruction enhance learning?](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021017),
  *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 103(1), 1–18. Their meta-analyses distinguish
  unassisted discovery from discovery supported by feedback, examples, scaffolding,
  and elicited explanations. This motivates allowing explanations and worked
  examples when questioning alone is not helping.
- **Adaptive scaffolding:** van de Pol, Volman, and Beishuizen (2010),
  [Scaffolding in teacher–student interaction: A decade of research](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-010-9127-6),
  *Educational Psychology Review*, 22, 271–296. The review identifies support matched
  to understanding, fading, and transfer of responsibility as key characteristics.
  This motivates adjusting help to the learner rather than counting failed attempts.
- **Feedback:** Hattie and Timperley (2007),
  [The power of feedback](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487),
  *Review of Educational Research*, 77(1), 81–112. The review distinguishes feedback
  about the task, process, and self-regulation from personal evaluation. This
  motivates specific feedback about reasoning and next steps rather than empty praise.
- **Retrieval practice:** Karpicke and Roediger (2008),
  [The critical importance of retrieval for learning](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408),
  *Science*, 319(5865), 966–968. In a vocabulary-learning experiment, repeated
  retrieval improved delayed recall. This supports retaining later recall checks;
  its results do not establish mastery across all subjects.

The uncertain skill estimates, omission of an overall percentage, transparent
challenges, and learner-controlled stopping are design choices. These citations
do not directly test those interface choices. The levels are an informal tracking
scheme, not a validated assessment instrument.
